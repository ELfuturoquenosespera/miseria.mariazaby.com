01 / THE STORY

“There are miseries that only success makes possible”

A narrator who implicates himself as he implicates others.

Alonso does not denounce corruption from the outside: he belongs to the world he judges. The tension stems from that contradiction, from his clear-eyed view of its rot and everything he is still willing to protect.

“Alonso Vega is a character who infuriates, amuses and fascinates in equal measure. A fierce novel, impossible to put down”

THE WORLD OF MISERIA

Every empire comes at a price.

Some pay in money. Others pay with their conscience.

A novel of sustained narrative tension and sharp psychological insight, where humour and cruelty expose what good manners so often conceal.

Miseria is a portrait of our times: of the decline and moral decay of Spain and Europe today, reaching far beyond politics or any single social class.

02 / THE WORLD

An acid view of the world, told by a man who turns on others the same cruelty he directs at himself.

He claims no moral high ground. And he spares nothing.

Miseria depicts a world of immaculate appearances and privately devastated lives. Its characters force us to ask what happens when a broken person reaches the limit: whether the final fall destroys them, or whether that is precisely when redemption becomes possible.

With lucid, brilliant and unsparing prose, María Zabay gives Alonso Vega de Saliente a corrosive, unsettling and at times irresistibly funny voice. It recalls the moral ferocity of Houellebecq and Martin Amis’s John Self, yet is firmly rooted in the business and family elites of contemporary Spain.

SYNOPSIS

To inherit an empire and discover its cost.

“When everything can be bought, the truth becomes the most dangerous asset”

Alonso Vega de Saliente, heir to the PUENTIA infrastructure empire, faces the sudden death of his father. Amid wealth and cynicism, the patriarch’s passing unleashes revelations about the murky origins of the family fortune.

Between a hostile takeover bid, investigations by Spain’s Civil Guard and relationships in which desire is another form of power, Alonso discovers that corruption infects personal bonds as surely as financial statements.

05 / THE CHARACTERS

They all orbit power.

No one comes away unscathed.

The heir

A man who has everything. Except control.

Alonso looks at the world with sarcasm and contempt, yet belongs to the very world he judges. Intelligent, privileged and used to giving orders, he begins to discover that inheriting an empire also means inheriting what made it possible.

What he fears: becoming too much like his father.

07 / THE AUTHOR

María Zabay.

Born in Zaragoza, María Zabay holds a law degree and is a writer and journalist. Her career spans print, radio and television, including presenting programmes on TVE, TVE2 and channels in the Mediaset group. She was also head of content in Spain for the international production company Digital TV Group.

A former communications director at Spain’s Ministry of Industry, Energy and Tourism, she has published eight novels to date—including El zapato de la lengua rota, La alumna, Aislada and Valentina—and co-authored three books on management. Manhattan won the Válgame Dios Literary Prize for Erotic Fiction.

She has written for publications including The Times, La Gaceta, Cinco Días, La Razón and OKDiario, where she currently hosts El Foco, a programme of interviews with figures from culture, politics, science and intellectual life.
